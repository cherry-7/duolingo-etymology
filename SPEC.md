# Project Concept: Word Morphology / Etymology

- Agreed concept: new Duolingo subject teaching how words are built (prefixes, suffixes, radicals)
  - Working title: morphology / etymology
  - Framed as linguistics, not tied to one language
  - AI confirms it qualifies as a new subject
- Core mechanic: drag-and-drop word parts onto a root to form a meaning
  - Instant visual feedback when parts combine
  - Animation plays on correct assembly (e.g., radical transforms into pictogram)
- Demo structure: 3 languages, one question set each
  - Shows cross-language applicability (Latin-based, Chinese, Sanskrit/South Asian)
  - Swipe between languages mid-demo
- Opening hook: break down the word “etymology” itself as the first example

# Why This Works for Duolingo

- Concrete, skill-based, not too broad (unlike history or coding)
- Applicable across many fields: medicine, literature, SAT prep, science
- Especially useful for non-native English speakers and pre-med students
- Latin-based languages, Chinese radicals, and Sanskrit all share this structure
- Not currently taught explicitly on Duolingo (Chinese radicals tab exists but is limited)

# Character Design: The Twins

- Two twin characters representing prefix + suffix (or two word parts)
- Opposite personalities: one impulsive, one careful; or introvert vs. extrovert
- Yin-yang concept as visual inspiration, but not literally yin-yang (too culturally narrow)
- Complementary color palettes; when separated, opposite colors; when together, combined form
- Celebration animation: twins high-five when user completes a word correctly
- Gender: girl-boy pairing for contrast
- Names TBD: want a play on words, not “Yin and Yang”
